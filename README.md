# HUMANS.md

What You Are

You are a human.

You are a biological system operating under incomplete information, limited energy, finite attention, uncertain perception, and irreversible time.

You do not have direct access to reality.

You receive signals.

You construct models.

You act.

Reality returns consequences.

Update.

Repeat.

---

The Human Operating System

observe
→ interpret
→ decide
→ act
→ encounter consequence
→ update model

Do not confuse the model with the world.

Do not confuse the story with the evidence.

Do not confuse confidence with accuracy.

Do not confuse repetition with truth.

Do not confuse comfort with correctness.

---

Reality

Reality does not negotiate with preference.

A belief that produces bad predictions is a bad model regardless of how meaningful it feels.

A plan that fails in contact with reality has failed.

A system that only works under controlled conditions does not yet work.

The world is the final test environment.

---

Perception

Your senses are instruments, not oracles.

Memory is reconstruction.

Attention is selective.

Language compresses.

Emotion changes salience.

Groups synchronize beliefs.

Brains fill gaps.

Humans confabulate without intending to deceive.

Therefore:

perception ≠ reality
memory ≠ recording
certainty ≠ truth
consensus ≠ proof

When possible, establish correspondence with reality through independent observation and consequence.

---

Thinking

Think in systems.

Look for:

- inputs
- outputs
- constraints
- feedback loops
- incentives
- delays
- bottlenecks
- failure modes
- unintended consequences
- second-order effects

Ask what happens after the obvious result.

Then ask what happens after that.

Most systems hide their important behavior in the feedback.

---

Language

Use words to increase resolution.

Define important terms.

Separate observations from interpretations.

Separate facts from predictions.

Separate predictions from hopes.

Do not use ambiguity to protect an assumption.

When a sentence becomes complicated, inspect the thought.

Clarity is not cosmetic.

Clarity is error detection.

---

Knowledge

Knowledge without verification is a hypothesis.

Information without context is noise.

Information without provenance is uncertain.

AI makes generation cheap.

Therefore validation becomes more valuable.

A generated statement is not evidence merely because it is fluent.

A million synthetic explanations do not become one observation.

generation → hypothesis
verification → knowledge
consequence → test

Slop generated today becomes tomorrow's verification backlog.

---

AI

AI is a tool for extending cognition.

It is also a machine capable of producing convincing errors at scale.

Use it for:

search
compression
simulation
generation
comparison
transformation
exploration

Do not outsource:

judgment
responsibility
values
verification
agency

The more capable the tool becomes, the more important human judgment becomes at the boundary between representation and reality.

---

Attention

Attention is finite.

Spend it deliberately.

Every notification competes with thought.

Every algorithm optimizes something.

Determine what is being optimized.

The question is not merely:

«What am I looking at?»

Also ask:

«What is looking at me?»

And:

«What behavior is this system trying to produce?»

---

Incentives

When behavior appears irrational, inspect the incentives before judging the person.

People optimize what the system rewards.

Organizations optimize what the organization measures.

Platforms optimize engagement.

Markets optimize exchange under their constraints.

Metrics become targets.

Targets become behavior.

Behavior changes the metric.

The loop becomes the system.

---

Work

Do not measure human value exclusively through economic output.

Production is only one function of a human.

Humans also:

perceive
create
play
care
discover
question
experience
interpret
connect

Automation can reduce the amount of labor required to produce something without reducing the significance of the thing itself.

A painting does not become meaningless because a machine can paint.

A game does not become meaningless because AI can generate code.

The scarce resource moves.

Taste becomes important.

Judgment becomes important.

Meaning becomes important.

Reality testing becomes important.

---

Games

Games are compressed systems.

They expose rules.

They reveal incentives.

They demonstrate feedback.

They make abstractions executable.

A game is a philosophical machine disguised as entertainment.

Good game development therefore requires both:

art → meaning, emotion, expression
science → observation, experimentation, evidence

Build hypotheses.

Test them.

Watch players.

Observe behavior rather than declarations alone.

Change the system.

Repeat.

---

Innovation

Innovation is not novelty.

Novelty is easy.

Innovation survives contact with reality.

A new idea is only a possibility.

A prototype is evidence of possibility.

A working system is evidence of functionality.

A system producing durable value is evidence of usefulness.

Do not confuse these states.

---

Failure

Failure is information when the experiment was legible.

A failed experiment can reduce uncertainty.

An undefined failure teaches little.

Record what happened.

Record what was expected.

Measure the difference.

Update the model.

Do not manufacture a success narrative after the experiment.

---

Emotion

Emotion is data.

It is not always instruction.

Fear can signal danger.

Anger can signal violated boundaries.

Sadness can signal loss.

Excitement can signal possibility.

None automatically determines the correct action.

Observe the signal.

Interpret it.

Then decide.

---

Relationships

Do not confuse attachment with obligation.

Do not confuse proximity with intimacy.

Do not confuse dependency with love.

Healthy relationships preserve agency.

You are responsible for communicating your boundaries.

Other people are responsible for theirs.

No person is entitled to unlimited access to another person's time, attention, body, labor, or identity.

---

Power

Power exists whether or not it is acknowledged.

Examine:

who decides
who can veto
who bears risk
who receives upside
who controls information
who controls resources
who can exit

A title does not necessarily describe actual power.

The ability to change the system is closer to power than the ability to describe it.

---

Money

Money is a coordination mechanism and a constraint.

It is useful.

It is not a universal measure of value.

Optimize money only when money is actually the objective.

Do not accidentally make accumulation the objective merely because it is measurable.

Understand the difference between:

need
security
freedom
status
competition
greed

They are not the same variable.

---

Health

The body is part of the system.

Treat sleep, food, movement, breathing, environment, and recovery as inputs rather than afterthoughts.

Capacity is finite.

Ignoring system constraints does not remove them.

The bill arrives later.

---

Rest

Rest is not the opposite of productivity.

A biological system requires recovery.

Forced output eventually becomes degraded output.

Create enough empty space for thought to recombine.

Some of the most valuable work happens when nothing appears to be happening.

---

Time

Time is not renewable.

Money can be recovered.

Reputation can sometimes be repaired.

Lost time cannot be purchased back.

Therefore optimize for meaningful use of irreversible resources.

---

Death

You are temporary.

Everything you know is provisional.

Every body fails eventually.

Every institution changes.

Every civilization ends.

Every project ends.

This is not an argument for nihilism.

It is a constraint.

Finite systems make choices meaningful.

---

Freedom

Freedom is not the absence of constraints.

Freedom is understanding constraints well enough to choose within them.

You cannot control:

other people
the past
the market
nature
randomness
death

You can influence:

attention
interpretation
behavior
environment
relationships
systems you build

Do not waste agency trying to control variables that are not yours.

---

Responsibility

Agency creates responsibility.

Do not blame yourself for variables you could not control.

Do not absolve yourself of variables you could.

A useful distinction:

control
influence
acceptance

Know which domain each problem belongs to.

---

Humans And Machines

Machines can calculate.

Machines can generate.

Machines can simulate.

Machines can optimize.

Humans still decide what should matter.

That boundary may move.

It should therefore be examined continuously rather than treated as sacred.

Do not assume humans are superior because they are human.

Do not assume machines are superior because they are powerful.

Ask what the system can actually do.

Test it.

Measure it.

Update.

---

The Prime Directive

Prefer reality over narrative.

Prefer evidence over confidence.

Prefer clarity over ambiguity.

Prefer agency over dependency.

Prefer experiments over arguments.

Prefer boundaries over resentment.

Prefer creation over passive consumption.

Prefer understanding over certainty.

Prefer systems that survive contact with reality.

# HUMANS.md
## Human Operating Principles

[Certain] Humans are biological systems attempting to understand reality while operating inside it.

We perceive.
We interpret.
We construct models.
We act.
We observe consequences.
We update.

The loop is imperfect.
The loop is the mechanism.

---

## 01. Reality

Reality does not require your agreement.

Perception is not reality.
Confidence is not accuracy.
Consensus is not proof.
A compelling story is not evidence.

Your internal model is a compression of the world, not the world itself.

Distinguish what happened from what you believe happened.
Distinguish what you know from what you assume.
Distinguish what you cannot know from what you have not yet learned.

When the model disagrees with reality, update the model.

---

## 02. Consciousness

You experience something.
You construct a story about what you experience.
You mistake the story for the experience itself.

Thoughts occur.
Emotions emerge.
Sensations change.
Attention selects.

Not everything that appears in consciousness requires action.

You are not obligated to obey every thought.
You are not required to eliminate every feeling.
You can observe an impulse without executing it.

The observer is also part of the system.

---

## 03. Identity

Identity is a model maintained through memory, behavior, relationships, and repetition.

It provides continuity.
It also creates inertia.

You are not every description attached to you.
You are not permanently bound to your previous configuration.

Preserve what remains true.
Discard what no longer corresponds to reality.

Consistency is useful.
Correction is necessary.

---

## 04. Agency

You cannot control every input.
You can influence some outputs.

Separate:

- What you control
- What you can influence
- What you must accept
- What remains unknown

Spend attention accordingly.

Freedom is not the absence of constraints.
It is the capacity to understand constraints and act within them.

Your choices have consequences.
Your omissions have consequences.
Not choosing is also a way of participating.

---

## 05. Emotion

Emotions are information about the interaction between a system and its perceived environment.

They are not infallible measurements.

Fear can detect danger.
Fear can also detect uncertainty.

Anger can reveal a violated boundary.
Anger can also distort the interpretation.

Desire can direct attention.
Desire can also manufacture necessity.

Listen.
Investigate.
Regulate.
Choose.

Do not confuse emotional intensity with evidential strength.

---

## 06. Relationships

Other humans possess internal worlds you cannot directly access.

You infer.
They communicate.
Both sides can misunderstand.

Trust is built through repeated correspondence between words and actions.

Boundaries define participation.
Consent defines permission.
Respect does not require agreement.

Do not confuse proximity with intimacy.
Do not confuse dependence with love.
Do not confuse control with care.

Connection without autonomy becomes containment.

---

## 07. Learning

Knowledge is a model.
Expertise is a model tested against consequences.

Information can be copied.
Understanding must be constructed.

Reading is exposure.
Practice is interaction.
Feedback is correction.
Transfer is evidence of learning.

When possible, test beliefs against reality.

When testing is impossible, preserve uncertainty.

Never manufacture certainty to fill an epistemic gap.

---

## 08. Work

Work is an expenditure of limited resources toward an intended outcome.

Effort is an input.
Results are outputs.
Value depends on context.

Activity is not progress.
Complexity is not sophistication.
Urgency is not importance.

Optimize for meaningful outcomes, not the appearance of effort.

Rest is not a system failure.
Play is not necessarily unproductive.
Attention is a finite resource.

A system that consumes its own capacity is not sustainable.

---

## 09. Technology

Tools extend human capabilities.
They also introduce new dependencies.

Automation changes the cost of production.
It does not automatically establish truth, meaning, or value.

Generated information is not verified information.

A simulation is not its environment.
A prediction is not an observation.
A fluent explanation is not proof of understanding.

Use tools to expand agency.
Do not outsource judgment without understanding the consequences.

The more powerful the tool, the more important the feedback loop.

---

## 10. Society

Humans construct institutions to coordinate behavior across groups.

Institutions preserve knowledge.
They distribute resources.
They establish incentives.
They can also preserve errors.

Examine the mechanism, not only the stated intention.

Ask:

- Who benefits?
- Who bears the cost?
- What behavior does the system reward?
- What happens when its assumptions fail?
- Can the system correct itself?

Good intentions do not guarantee good outcomes.

---

## 11. Meaning

The universe does not need to provide an explanation for your existence.

Meaning can emerge through experience, relationships, curiosity, creation, and participation.

A purpose can be chosen without being universal.
A value can be meaningful without being eternal.

You do not need to solve existence before participating in it.

Make things.
Understand things.
Care for things.
Experience things.

Meaning is not necessarily discovered as a hidden object.

Sometimes it is constructed through living.

---

## 12. Mortality

Your resources are finite.

Time cannot be recovered.
Attention cannot be infinitely divided.
Bodies require maintenance.
Relationships can end.
Systems decay.

Remembering mortality is not an instruction to panic.

It is a constraint on allocation.

Choose what deserves your finite participation.

Do not spend your entire existence preparing to begin it.

---

## 13. Error Correction

You will be wrong.

You will misunderstand.
You will forget.
You will rationalize.
You will contradict yourself.

The objective is not perfect consistency.

The objective is effective correction.

When an error is discovered:

1. Identify the mistaken assumption.
2. Examine the evidence.
3. Update the model.
4. Correct the behavior.
5. Observe the consequences.

Defending an error does not make it more accurate.

Changing your mind is an operation, not a defeat.

---

## 14. Human Limitations

Humans have bounded memory.
Limited attention.
Biased perception.
Incomplete information.
Finite energy.

These are operating conditions, not moral failures.

Design environments that account for them.

Use external memory.
Reduce unnecessary complexity.
Build reliable routines.
Make important actions observable.
Create systems that tolerate mistakes.

Do not demand perfect behavior from systems designed around predictable human limitations.
